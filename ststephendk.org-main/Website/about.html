About Our Parish

Learn about our history, mission, and the people who make our community special

Welcome to St. Stephen Parish

A vibrant Catholic community in Darkuman, Accra, dedicated to serving God and our neighbors

Our Mission

St. Stephen Parish exists to glorify God by making disciples who know Christ, grow in Christ, and go for Christ. We are committed to worship, fellowship, discipleship, ministry, and evangelism.

We strive to be a beacon of hope and faith in our community, serving the spiritual and physical needs of all people with love and compassion.

Our Vision

To be a thriving, Christ-centered community where all people can encounter the living God, experience transformative worship, and be equipped to serve others with the love of Christ.

We envision a parish that is deeply rooted in Catholic tradition while being relevant and responsive to the needs of our contemporary society.

Our History

The Genesis

St. Stephen Catholic Church is an offshoot of the Holy Family Parish, Mataheko. The idea to establish a Church in the Darkuman Community originated from a crusade launched in 1977 by the then Parish Priest of the Holy Family Parish, Rev. Fr. Paul Van Riel, an SVD, for the spread of the Catholic faith in the suburbs of the city of Accra. At that time the Holy Family Parish served as a central point where the faithful from the suburbs converged to worship.

This served as a driving force for the establishment of community-based churches in areas such as Darkuman, Kaneshie, Dansoman, Odorkor to afford easy access to worship, propagation of the Gospel and spread of the Catholic faith. A few parishioners resident at Darkuman responded positively to the call as it did happened in other communities mentioned above. Mr. Stephen Ocloo led the crusade to form the St. Stephen's Catholic Church in March, 1978.

Naming of the Church

It is worthy of note that the name "Stephen" was given to the Church in 1982, in honour of two of its prominent personalities in the person of Mr. Stephen Ocloo and Stephen Kwame Kersi. The pioneers included:

Inauguration and Early Development

The inaugural ceremony of the founding of the New Church took place at the residence of Mr. Stephen Ocloo, which also served as the meeting place for a period of three months. Thereafter, the Church relocated to "Brisco House" where they met and worshipped for a period of one year and then returned to the residence of Mr. Ocloo, and worshipped there for another year.

The sturdy membership growth demanded a more suitable place of worship and with the kind permission of the late Mr. Samuel Kwesi Newman, a staunch Catholic and proprietor of the then New Royal International School, the congregation was allocated one of the classrooms in 1980, and congregated and worshipped there till 1982. In the same year, the Church saw the need to acquire a permanent place of worship. Consequently, the late Mr. Stephen Kersi donated a piece of land for the intended purpose.

Leadership History

First Chairman - Mr. Stephen Ocloo (1978-1982)

Mr. Stephen Ocloo remained the leader of the Church from the day of its foundation until 1982 with an estimated membership strength of two hundred and fifty from the initial fourteen pioneers. The need for a permanent place of worship and pastoral support became imperative and in 1981 Rev. Fr. Charles Rosaline was posted as a visiting Priest from the Charles Lwanga Parish, Abeka to assist in the pastoral work of the Church.

In 1982, the Church members constructed a small chapel on that piece of land for the celebration of Mass. Masses were celebrated in the small chapel until somewhere in 2000 when the congregation moved into a new chapel which was under construction.

Second Chairman - Mr. Samuel Kwesi Newman (1983-1987)

Mr. Samuel Kwesi Newman, the then proprietor of the New Royal International School, Darkuman, succeeded Mr. Stephen Ocloo as Chairman of the Church Pastoral Council. During his administration, a makeshift structure was erected on the land donated by Mr. Stephen Kersi. Notably, Mr. Newman provided the Church Choir with its first robes.

Third Chairman - Mr. Peter Akwetea (1987-1990)

Under Mr. Akwetea's leadership, the Church saw significant developments:

  • Extended the existing Church structure, increasing seating capacity from 300 to 500
  • Acquired additional adjoining land through Mr. Stephen Kersi's help
  • Secured the property with a fence wall built through communal labor

During this period, Rev. Fr. Anthony Balee, a representative of the Pontifical Missionary Services (P.M.S) in Ghana, succeeded Rev. Fr. Rosaline as the Priest-in-Charge (1982-1992).

Fourth Chairman - Mr. Matthew Kofi Koomson (1990-1995)

Mr. Koomson's administration, the longest and most eventful, laid a solid foundation for transforming the St. Stephen's community. His achievements included:

  • Establishing various Church Pastoral Council Committees
  • Renovating the existing Church building
  • Preparing building plans for a new Church building, rectory, and school
  • Installing drainage systems to prevent flooding
  • Constructing sanitary facilities and a warehouse

The New Church Building Project (1990-1995)

In 1990, the Church Pastoral Council set up a Building Committee to produce a block plan shortly after its inauguration. The committee co-opted Mr. Patrick Adjakpo, a parishioner and draftsman, to assist in the work of the committee.

A fundraising initiative for the new Church building was launched, yielding ¢600,000.00 against the estimated total project cost of ¢60,000,000.00. Despite the modest initial response, the event successfully:

  • Created awareness of the new administration's objectives
  • Raised consciousness about the required sacrifices from the congregation
  • Initiated the journey toward the cherished dream of a new Church building

In 1992, through Rev. Msgr. Balee's efforts, the Pontifical Missionary Services (P.M.S) made a significant contribution of $20,000.00 toward the project. After completing the architectural and structural drawings and resolving various challenges, particularly regarding the building's siting, construction of the foundation began in 1995.

The project's oversight transitioned to the next administration, led by the late Mr. Albert Kofi Adubofour, on February 28, 1995. Since then, successive administrations have maintained the continuity of this significant undertaking.

The Development of St. Stephen's Catholic School

The idea of establishing St. Stephen's Catholic School originated from the Sunday school operations that were in place when Mr. Adubofour's administration took office. Initially, the plan was to create a Day Care Centre as an extension of the Sunday school, primarily to benefit Catholic children by providing uninterrupted Catholic training and to address concerns about Charismatic teachers in the Sunday school.

Background Challenges

At that time, the Catholic Charismatic Renewal was affiliated with similar organizations based in Legon that opposed certain Catholic practices, particularly the use of the Rosary and statues in churches. There were concerning incidents, including:

  • An ultimatum issued to the Holy Spirit Cathedral in May 1988 demanding the removal of statues and sacramentals
  • Alleged incidents where Rosaries were collected from Catholic students and publicly burned during outreach programs at non-denominational educational institutions

These concerns led to widespread discontent among Pastoral Councilors about the Sunday School's management by Charismatic teachers, fearing their ability to properly impart Catholic doctrines to children.

Formation of the Education Committee

The Education Committee was formed to address these concerns and held its first meeting on March 12, 1991, to establish objectives. The committee consisted of:

  • • Mr. Matthew Kofi Koomson (deceased) - Chairman
  • • Theresa Abbeyquaye (Mrs.) - Secretary/Treasurer
  • • Cecilia Sakyiamah (Mrs.) (deceased) - Member
  • • S.K. Newman (deceased) - Member
  • • Anthony Kukubor (deceased) - Member
  • • John Dickson Sarpeh - Member
  • • Elizabeth Asiedua - Member
Objectives and Implementation

The committee set the following objectives:

  • Establish a Day Care Centre for St. Stephen Catholic Church children and admit non-Catholics whose parents would abide by Catholic rules
  • Assist kindergarten graduates in securing admissions to reputable Catholic Schools until the Church could absorb them into its own system
  • Mobilize resources for establishing a formal education system in the future

The Day Care Centre's resource needs were identified as manpower, accommodation, teaching aids, recreational aids, and toilet facilities. Mrs. Cecilia Sakyiamah was appointed Head Teacher in late 1992, followed by the engagement of teaching staff. With Church funds, a two-classroom block with facilities for a store and office was built, and the Day Care Centre formally opened on January 12, 1993, with the motto: "Knowledge, Truth and Service."

Foreign Aid

In 1991, during the Education Committee's preparations, Msgr. Anthony Balee returned from Rome with news of a potential financial assistance offer from a Swedish NGO through his friend, Mr. Berut Kalseen from Stockholm. Although the initial offer for vocational training didn't align with the Church's plans, they applied for support for formal education.

The application was submitted to Sweden on September 9, 1991. While the Day Care Centre idea was enthusiastically welcomed by the community, the school project faced resistance due to:

  • Limited land space for both school and Church building projects
  • Concerns that Church funds would be diverted to the school project, slowing progress on the Church community's priorities

The funding application was successful, with Caritas Sweden becoming the benefactor. The initial remittance of $22,000.00 was received in 1992 but wasn't disbursed until December 1994, when the school building project's foundation began. Caritas has remained the primary financier, with normal support from the Church.

Succession of Parish Pastoral Council Chairmen

Mr. Jacob Mensah Demitia (1997-2001)

Mr. Mensah Demitia was elected chairman in November 1997 and served until 2001. During his tenure, the Chapel block works were carried out.

Mr. Edward Taylor (2001-2006)

Mr. Edward Taylor took office as chairman in 2001, with his first term ending in 2004. He was re-elected for a second term, which ended in 2006. During his administration:

  • Decoration, ceiling, and painting works of the chapel were undertaken

Mr. John Kofi Agama (2006-2008)

Mr. John Kofi Agama's administration began in 2006 and ended in 2008.

Mr. Dennis Aggrey-Ampiah (2008-2010)

Mr. John Kuma Cato (2010-2014)

Mr. John Kuma Cato succeeded Mr. Dennis Aggrey-Ampiah in 2010. He was re-elected in 2012 and completed his tenure in 2014. His administration's achievements included:

  • Refurbishment of the Conference Room
  • Initiation of the Mission House construction
Mr. Alfred Koomson (2015-2016)

Mr. Alfred Koomson succeeded Mr. John Kuma Cato in 2015 and served until 2016. During his administration:
